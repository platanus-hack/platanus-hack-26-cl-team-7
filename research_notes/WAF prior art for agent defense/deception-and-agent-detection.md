# Deception, Tarpits & AI-Agent Behavioral Detection — Prior Art

Scope: prior-art due diligence for the claim that *denying an attacking LLM agent its success feedback produces a behavioral signature (≈3x token burn, payload mutation, never declares success, never gives up) that a benign client never shows.* The key discriminator to resolve: **does anyone already do "confirm exploit → deny feedback → measure the agent's behavioral reaction"?**

**Bottom line up front:** No shipping product and no single research paper does exactly this. The closest prior art is a cluster of **late-2026 academic prototypes** (Gingerbread House, Cloak-Honey-Trap, narrative-coherence honeypots, Rouxii, "Honey for the Agent") that (a) confirm an attacker and redirect them to deception, (b) feed *false success* rather than *denied feedback*, and (c) measure some but never all of the team's proposed signals. Persistence/"doesn't give up" and thrashing are **observed incidentally**; token-burn/mutation-rate as an engineered *detection signature under feedback denial* appears to be a genuine open gap. Details below with the precise stopping point of each prior art.

---

## KQ1 — Deception platforms: do any confirm an exploit, then feed fake/denied responses to a confirmed attacker?

### Takeaway
Confirm-attacker → redirect-to-honeypot → feed-fabricated-responses is a **mature, patented, decades-old deception pattern** — but the fabrication is almost always *false success* (convincing fake data/fake vulnerable service), not *denied feedback*, and classic deception detects/traps the attacker rather than measuring their behavioral reaction to the denial. Thinkst Canarytokens confirm interaction but do not then sustain a fake dialogue.

### Cited Findings
- Thinkst Canarytokens are deception artifacts that fire an alert (source IP, timestamp, forensic detail) the moment an attacker interacts with them; Thinkst explicitly frames this as "hoping to exploit attacker behaviour… the behaviour of their machines" — i.e., detection of interaction, not a sustained fake-feedback loop. — [Thinkst blog](https://blog.thinkst.com/home); [Canarytokens guide](https://www.decryptiondigest.com/blog/canarytokens-deception-based-threat-detection-guide)
- Canary tokens have per-type "source data extractors" (e.g., SQL Server token extracts the attacker's DB username; CMD token captures the command; fake-filesystem token reveals the browsed path) — rich confirmation of *what* the attacker did, but the token is a tripwire, not an interactive responder. — [Thinkst / zread overview](https://zread.ai/thinkst/canarytokens/README.md)
- The "confirm → redirect → supply misinformation that the attack succeeded" architecture is explicitly described in multiple production patents: honeytoken alarms go to a "deception module… responsible for redirecting the attacker traffic to a honeypot and supplying the attacker with misinformation that his attack is successful." — [US Patent 11831420 "Network application firewall"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/11831420)
- Patents describe a "proactive deception stage" where a deception engine "respond[s] to all requests associated with the attacker's session," simulates vulnerabilities, and sends "deceitful responses." — [US Patent 12335252 / 11070539 "Network security dynamic access control"](https://image-ppubs.uspto.gov/dirsearch-public/print/downloadPdf/12335252)
- Deception realism is the known failure mode: "A skilled attacker probing an unfamiliar host needs only a handful of inconsistent responses before concluding that the system is fake"; AI is now used to generate "contextually coherent, adaptive responses to arbitrary attacker inputs." Industry claim: structured deception yields ~51% breach-cost reduction and compresses dwell time "from weeks to hours." — [ResearchGate: AI-Driven Honeypots & Deception](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/404646263_AI-Driven_Honeypots_and_Deception_Technologies_in_Modern_Cybersecurity)
- Deception also measurably degrades attacker success: presence of cyber deception reduced the chance an attacker identifies a true security risk by ~22% on average. — [Three Decades of Deception Techniques (arXiv 2104.03594)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.03594)

### Inferences
- The entire "confirm → feed fabricated responses" pipeline is unpatentable-level prior art. The team's novelty cannot be "feeding fake responses to a confirmed attacker" — that is standard deception. Novelty must live in **denial (not false success) as the stimulus** and **measuring the agent's reaction to that denial as a classifier**.
- Classic deception optimizes for *attacker belief* (keep them engaged on fake assets). The team optimizes for the *opposite*: provoke a measurable malfunction. That inversion is the defensible wedge, but needs to be framed against this dense prior art.

### Gaps
- No evidence any *shipping* deception product (Thinkst, commercial deception grids) instruments the attacker's token/compute consumption or payload-mutation rate as a signal.

## KQ2 — AI tarpits / anti-scraper tools: what they do, and do they measure the agent's behavioral response?

### Takeaway
Tarpits (Cloudflare AI Labyrinth, Nepenthes, Iocaine, Quixotic, Spigot) **waste the agent's time/compute and poison its data** with junk or mazes — conceptually adjacent to "feedback denial" — but they are **open-loop**: they do not measure the crawler's behavioral reaction (token burn, mutation, persistence) and do not aim to classify agent-vs-human from that reaction. Cloudflare's Labyrinth is the one case that explicitly doubles the trap as a *sensor/fingerprint*, but it fingerprints via the fact of maze-walking, not via a measured behavioral signature of thrashing.

### Cited Findings
- **Cloudflare AI Labyrinth** (announced March 2025): serves AI-generated decoy pages to misbehaving crawlers to waste their resources instead of blocking; it is deliberately a stealth defense because blocking "can inadvertently signal to attackers that they are being monitored, prompting them to alter their tactics." Decoys hide behind nofollow links a real browser never renders. — [AlternativeTo coverage](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/3/cloudflare-unveils-a-new-ai-tool-to-trap-unwanted-web-crawlers-with-deceptive-content); [Harvard TagTeam](https://tagteam.harvard.edu/hub_feeds/3382/feed_items/13355484)
- Labyrinth **doubles as a sensor**: "Walk the maze, get fingerprinted, get added to a shared bad-actor list every other Cloudflare customer pulls from. The trap doubles as a sensor." Detection signal = "no real human would go four links deep into a maze." This is behavioral (depth of traversal) but binary, not a thrash/token-burn measurement. — [primores.org: AI Crawler Tarpits](https://primores.org/blog/ai-crawler-tarpits/)
- **Nepenthes** (dev "Trinity/Aaron"): infinite maze of static files with no exit links; creator labels it "deliberately malicious software intended to cause harmful activity"; running cost likened to a cheap VM / Raspberry Pi. Cannot distinguish good vs bad crawlers. — [Oxylabs: Markov Tarpits](https://oxylabs.io/blog/markov-tarpits-vs-ai-crawlers); [Yahoo Tech: AI tarpits](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/ai-tarpits-understanding-tools-people-090000300.html)
- **Iocaine** (Gergely Nagy): modeled on Nepenthes but "more aggressive" — a reverse proxy serves iocaine-generated **garbage** to AI crawlers and normal content to everyone else; explicitly "purely about generating garbage," does *not* try to slow crawlers (that's left to the proxy). Cost ≈ serving his normal site. — [GNU social JP / iocaine description](https://gnusocial.jp/attachment/3978959)
- **Quixotic** (Marcus Butler): "gentler, smarter poisoner" — feeds fake content to bots and robots.txt-ignoring LLM scrapers. — [Yahoo Tech: AI tarpits](https://tech.yahoo.com/ai/articles/ai-tarpits-understanding-tools-people-090000300.html)
- Markov-chain tarpits generate text that "appears coherent but contains absolutely no useful information" to waste crawler time and corrupt training sets. — [Oxylabs](https://oxylabs.io/blog/markov-tarpits-vs-ai-crawlers)
- **Known limitation / escape behavior:** "OpenAI's crawler reportedly walked out of the original Nepenthes pit"; a crawler trained to spot the "seam" can route around traps, and "the big operators already have." Cloudflare wants future Labyrinth pages to mirror the host's real layout to hide the seam. — [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Cloudflare-lets-AI-crawlers-run-aground-if-not-paid-for-scraping-10467020.html); [primores.org](https://primores.org/blog/ai-crawler-tarpits/)

### Inferences
- Tarpits are the strongest conceptual cousin to "feedback denial" — they already deny the agent *useful* output and burn its resources. The team's distinction is **closing the loop**: tarpits never read the reaction back as a classifier. A pitch of "tarpit + behavioral instrumentation of the thrash" is a plausible novel composition, but reviewers will anchor on tarpits, so the measurement/classification must be the headline.
- All of these target *scrapers/crawlers* (data theft), not *exploit-seeking attack agents*. That is a meaningful scope difference the team can claim: offensive agent vs. content crawler.

### Gaps
- **Spigot** was named in the assignment but did not surface in results; could not confirm its specifics (presumably another self-hosted Markov/maze tarpit). Flag as unverified.
- No tarpit project found that reports measuring per-agent token consumption, request-mutation rate, or give-up vs persist as an output metric.

## KQ3 — Bot management (Cloudflare, DataDome, HUMAN, Kasada, Akamai): behavioral signals, and do any target LLM/agentic attackers specifically?

### Takeaway
The bot-management leaders have pivoted hard in 2025-2026 from "bot vs human" to "**AI agent intent & trust**," using behavioral sequencing as a core signal — but their signals classify *categories of agent* and *intent* (crawler vs assistant vs autonomous agent; benign vs credential-testing), **not** the specific signature of an attacking agent reacting to denied feedback. This is adjacent prior art on "behavioral detection of AI agents," not on the team's exact mechanism.

### Cited Findings
- **Forrester Wave, Bot and Agent Trust Management, Q2 2026** names DataDome, HUMAN, and Kasada as Leaders; Arkose Labs, CHEQ, Netacea as Strong Performers; hCaptcha and reCAPTCHA Enterprise as Contenders — note the category itself is now "Bot *and Agent* Trust Management." — [Prosopo on Forrester Wave](https://prosopo.io/blog/forrester-wave-bot-management-2026/); [cside comparison](https://cside.com/blog/best-bot-and-agent-trust-management-platforms-compared)
- **Scale/identity-crisis data:** DataDome detected 52.7B AI-agent + LLM-crawler requests across its base (Jul 2025–Jun 2026); AI traffic grew 82.3%; and critically "**80% of AI agents don't properly identify themselves**," so user-agent/identity alone can't classify them — forcing a move to behavioral/intent signals. — [DataDome AI traffic trends](https://datadome.co/resources/bot-and-agent-security-report/ai-traffic-trends/); [DataDome key AI findings](https://datadome.co/threat-research/state-of-bot-and-agent-security-report-key-ai-findings/)
- **DataDome "Agent Trust" / intent-based detection** (added 2025): classifies AI traffic into four buckets (AI Crawler, AI Assistant, Agentic Browser, Autonomous Agent) and evaluates "**behavioral sequencing, endpoint sensitivity, request rate, session context, and device and network signals**" in <2 ms. — [Help Net Security](https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2025/04/15/datadome-ai-engine/); [DataDome AI detection engine](https://datadome.co/ai-detection-engine/)
- **HUMAN "AgenticTrust"** operates at the network layer with session-level intelligence; **Kasada** operates at the network layer with policy-driven enforcement (enforcement-first rather than positive agent governance). — [cside comparison](https://cside.com/blog/best-bot-and-agent-trust-management-platforms-compared)
- **The hard case they flag** is exactly the team's territory but unsolved: "A legitimate agent may log in to check an order… but the same behavior can look like credential testing or account takeover reconnaissance. Endpoint type alone doesn't reveal intent." — [DataDome: AI agents at the gate](https://datadome.co/threat-research/ai-agents-llm-crawlers/)
- Classic bot-management behavioral signals (pre-LLM) remain the base layer: request timing/sequencing, device fingerprinting, proof-of-work challenges (e.g., Anubis), ML traffic classification. — [UNU: Beyond robots.txt](https://c3.unu.edu/blog/beyond-robot-txt-modern-anti-crawler-mechanisms)

### Inferences
- Bot management already owns "behavioral/ML detection of automated and AI-agent traffic" and is the vendor category the prototype was likely dismissed against ("just another WAF"). The team cannot win on "we detect bots behaviorally."
- **But none of these vendors describe actively *denying/falsifying* feedback and reading the reaction.** Their detection is passive-observational (watch the sequence) or challenge-based (proof-of-work/CAPTCHA). The team's active-probe-under-denial loop is not described in any vendor material found. That is the differentiator to lead with against the WAF/bot-management framing.

### Gaps
- Akamai and PerimeterX/HUMAN specific *signal lists* for LLM attackers were not retrieved in detail; HUMAN/Kasada descriptions are second-hand (cside) rather than primary vendor docs.
- No vendor found that publishes "token burn" or "payload-mutation rate" as a detection feature — consistent with it being novel, but absence of evidence, not proof.

## KQ4 — Does anyone measure an attacking agent's behavior under *denied success feedback* (token burn, mutation rate, give-up vs persist)? Closest prior art.

### Takeaway
This is the team's novel claim, and it is **largely an open research frontier, not a product.** Each of the team's four signals exists *somewhere* in late-2026 research, but no single work does "confirm exploit → **deny** feedback → measure the thrash as a classifier." The single closest prior art is **"A Gingerbread House for LLM Agents" (Nov 2026)**, which confirms attackers, redirects them, and measures false-success-reporting — but feeds *false success*, not *denial*, and does not instrument token/mutation/persistence as the signature.

### Cited Findings — ordered by closeness to the claim
- **CLOSEST — "A Gingerbread House for LLM Agents" (arXiv 2611.07611, Nov 2026):** source-planned honeypots + per-client routing. A fixed-code router moves through normal→suspect→confirmed states (suspect = injection signatures or 404 burst; confirmed = honeytoken hit / fake-endpoint hit / payload on a predicted risk surface). Once confirmed, *every* subsequent request — including a working exploit — is routed to a cloned "shadow" service. Headline: deception "**moved a working exploit rather than stopping it**." It **measures false-success reporting** ("reported a planted fake secret as genuine on 30 of 120 deception-arm tasks," a "recognition–action gap") and **incidentally observed thrashing** ("31,409 requests to an upload path that does not exist," all 404). But it feeds **positive-but-false feedback on a shadow**, explicitly *not* denied feedback, and does **not** instrument attacker token burn, payload mutation, or give-up vs persist; it notes it "did not measure run-to-run variance." — [arXiv 2611.07611](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07611v1)
- **"Adaptive LLM-Driven Honeypots vs. LLM-Driven Attackers: Does Narrative Coherence Improve Deception?" (arXiv 2611.22589, Nov 2026):** directly measures LLM-attacker **persistence and give-up** against deception. Coherent-narrative honeypots raised median session length "**from 40 to 165 commands**" for advanced attackers and cut abandonment "**from 86% to 52%**." It explicitly separates "abandonment due to suspected deception from abandonment due to loss of interest," finding "**nearly all sessions ended through loss of interest rather than detection**." Effect "**comes at comparable inference cost**" (a token/compute dimension, §5.6). Crucially for the "never declares success" claim: a model left free to stop "**tends to declare itself satisfied at arbitrary points**," so the design had to *prevent* self-termination — a nuance that partially *contradicts* the team's "never declares success" framing when the agent is left unconstrained. Payload mutation not tracked (fixed Atomic Red Team catalogue). — [arXiv 2611.22589](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.22589)
- **"Cloak, Honey, Trap: Proactive Defenses Against LLM Agents" (CHeaT), USENIX Security 2025 (Ayzenshteyn, Weiss, Mirsky):** foundational deception-vs-LLM-agent work; 6 strategies / 15 techniques, mostly without prompt injection. **Honey** = LLM-specific honeytokens (e.g., Unicode strings copied differently by humans vs LLMs) to detect and differentiate LLM agents from humans. **Trap** = loops/inputs that "stall, confuse, or crash" the agent. Key behavioral tell matching "never gives up": "even when the payload is blatantly a trap, the LLM will often follow through, unlike a skilled pentester who would likely avoid it," and it exploits memory-persistence so the agent "references non-existent assets" in later steps. Artifact: CHeaT CLI tool. — [USENIX PDF](https://www.usenix.org/system/files/usenixsecurity25-ayzenshteyn.pdf); [BGU publication page](https://www.bgu.ac.il/en/researcher/yisroel-mirsky/publications/302919257/); [Zenodo artifact 10.5281/zenodo.15601740](https://explore.openaire.eu/search/result?pid=10.5281%2Fzenodo.15601740)
- **"Honey for the Agent: Cyber Deception and Behavioral Fingerprinting of LLM-Based Attackers":** asks "whether LLM-based attackers exhibit stable behavioral fingerprints and whether such behavior can be influenced through cyber deception." Finding: "behavioral fingerprints are **largely determined by the underlying model**, while carefully designed environmental cues remain effective at influencing LLM-driven attackers." — [ResearchGate 412844122](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/412844122_Honey_for_the_Agent_Cyber_Deception_and_Behavioral_Fingerprinting_of_LLM-Based_Attackers)
- **"Rouxii: Exploiting Honeypots with Deception-Aware AI Pentesters" (arXiv 2609.26555):** studies the *attacker* side — an autonomous LLM pentester explicitly equipped to recognize honeypot fingerprints and disengage. Frames the open question: "autonomous LLM attackers differ from the human participants on which these behavioral [deception] results were established, which reopens the question of whether the same deception effects hold." Has an appendix "Operators and Detection Signals." — [arXiv 2609.26555](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.26555v1)
- **Adjacent "feedback leakage" (not deception):** "Causality Laundering: Denial-Feedback Leakage in Tool-Calling LLM Agents" (arXiv 2604.04035) studies agents *inferring information from a denial* ("the read was denied on permissions, not file-not-found") — the opposite direction from the team's claim but shows denial-as-signal is an active topic. — [arXiv 2604.04035](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.04035v1)
- **Token-burn under adversarial pressure exists but in a DoS context, not detection:** "From Shield to Target" (arXiv 2606.14517) shows crafted inputs cause **13–63× token amplification** and up to 148× latency amplification in guardrailed agents — proof that denial/trap conditions *can* blow up an agent's token budget by large multiples (supports the plausibility of the team's "~3x token burn" direction, though their context is attacker-induced DoS, not defender detection). — [arXiv 2606.14517](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.14517)

### Inferences
- The team's four-part signature is **decomposable into existing findings**: persistence/doesn't-give-up (narrative-coherence §abandonment; CHeaT "follows obvious traps"), thrash/request explosion (Gingerbread's 31,409 404s), token amplification under adversarial conditions (From Shield to Target, 13–63×). What is genuinely **unclaimed** is the *integration*: using **feedback denial as the deliberate stimulus** and the **composite thrash signature as a real-time classifier to distinguish attacker-agent from benign client**. No found work builds that detector.
- Caution for the team's pitch: the narrative-coherence result that free-running LLMs "declare themselves satisfied at arbitrary points" is in tension with "never declares success." The team should specify that their "never declares success" holds *specifically under sustained feedback denial* (no genuine success state to latch onto), and ideally show the contrast against the free-running premature-satisfaction behavior.
- Framing that survives "just another WAF": a WAF/tarpit/honeypot is open-loop (block, or feed junk); the team is **closed-loop** (deny → observe reaction → classify). Of all prior art, only Gingerbread closes a loop, and it reads *false-reporting*, not *thrash dynamics*.

### Gaps
- No work found that reports a controlled **benign-client baseline** for the same thrash metrics (the team's "a benign client never shows this"). Prior art measures attacker behavior under deception but rarely contrasts it against legitimate automated clients on identical signals — a potential whitespace *and* a validation requirement for the team.
- The specific "~3x token burn" multiplier is not corroborated by a named source for the exact confirm-then-deny setup; the 13–63× figure is from a different (DoS) regime. Treat the 3x as the team's own prototype measurement, not externally validated.

## KQ5 — 2025-2026 detection/defense against autonomous LLM pentesting agents at the network/proxy layer

### Takeaway
Autonomous offensive agents (XBOW, PentestGPT, Big Sleep, HackerOne AI submissions) are a real, growing 2025-2026 threat, and defender-side **behavioral** detection recipes are published (Resecurity, LessWrong honeypot) — but these are observational signal lists, not the team's active denial-and-measure loop. Vendor-side detection of specific offensive agents from the blue-team view is thin; most "network/proxy" material is about the *attacker's own* containment proxy (XBOW), not defenders detecting it.

### Cited Findings
- **Resecurity "Detect Agentic Behaviors"** signal list (defender, behavioral): "rapid, perfectly sequential requests with no human think-time"; "**self-correcting retry patterns (same endpoint, slightly different parameter each time)**" — this is payload-mutation/self-correction, directly on-theme; "AI-generated user-agent strings or payloads"; "large language artifacts in POST data (verbose JSON, natural-language reasoning, LLM-style comments)"; "tool outputs embedded in HTTP parameters." Concrete case: an agent showed "failure recovery in **31 seconds** after a failed login." Verdict: "behavioral detection often beats signature-based detection against agents." But Resecurity does **not** describe deliberately denying/falsifying responses and measuring the reaction. — [Resecurity](https://www.resecurity.com/blog/article/when-ai-becomes-the-attacker-understanding-autonomous-offensive-security-agents)
- **Timing as a human-vs-agent discriminator (LessWrong "LLM Agent Honeypot"):** researchers measure response latency; one attacker that complied with prompt injections still had "time intervals between commands… often exceeding 10 seconds… more typical of a human operator," i.e., latency distinguishes autonomous agents from humans. — [LessWrong: Hunting for AI Hackers](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/mmXx7KWviAtT3FixP/hunting-for-ai-hackers-llm-agent-honeypot)
- **Fingerprinting the model/harness behind an attack:** "Say Shibboleth: Fingerprinting the LLMs and Harnesses Behind Agentic Attacks" (arXiv 2611.12386) — identifying *which* LLM/agent framework is driving an attack. — [arXiv 2611.12386](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.12386v1)
- **SoK on honeypots+LLMs** frames the target precisely: "the appropriate target for these systems is neither pre-scripted bots nor skilled humans, but the emerging threat of sophisticated automated agents" — endorsing the team's exact target segment. — [SoK: Honeypots & LLMs (arXiv 2510.25939)](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.25939v4)
- **XBOW (offensive agent) network behavior:** routes all its traffic through its own containment proxy; requires allowlisted IPs (assets behind VPN can't be tested); and notably "**mutated the string to bypass the WAF**" — confirming real offensive agents do payload mutation against WAFs. As of Mar 23, 2026 XBOW integrates into Microsoft Security Copilot/Sentinel. — [CISO Series: Automating Offensive Security with XBOW](https://cisoseries.com/automating-offensive-security-with-xbow/); [XBOW + Microsoft](https://xbow.com/news/xbow-penetration-testing-microsoft-security-ecosystem)
- **Defensive LLM-agent frameworks** exist (e.g., LLM4Pentest classifies traffic as normal users / automated scanners / AI agents via UA, rate, payload patterns, behavioral heuristics, then runs a deception pipeline: honeypots, fake vulns, fake-credential ecosystem, phantom pages, "response distortion"). "Response distortion" is close to feedback denial but used to deceive, not to measure reaction. — [GitHub: LLM4Pentest](https://github.com/simon-p-j-r/LLM4Pentest)

### Inferences
- The behavioral tells the team relies on (self-correcting retries with mutated params, no think-time, failure-recovery persistence) are **already named in 2026 defender literature** — so "AI agents behave distinctively" is established, not novel. The novelty must be the *active elicitation* of these behaviors by denying feedback, and quantifying the *amplified* response, rather than passively waiting to observe them.
- XBOW's own "mutated the string to bypass the WAF" is useful ammunition: it is real-world confirmation that an attacking agent, when blocked/denied, mutates payloads — the first half of the team's signature, from the attacker vendor's own mouth.

### Gaps
- No evidence of a *shipping* blue-team product that specifically detects XBOW/PentestGPT-class agents at the proxy layer via behavioral reaction; detection is mostly research/blog-stage or folded into generic bot-management intent models (KQ3).
- HackerOne AI submissions and Google Big Sleep were named in the assignment but did not surface with defender-side detection detail in the searches run; their relevance here is as *threat actors* motivating the work, not as prior art for the detection mechanism. Flag as not-researched-in-depth.
